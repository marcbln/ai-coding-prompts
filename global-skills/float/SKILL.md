# Float

Generator-first ideation. A counterweight to `brainstorming`: that skill interrogates and converges; this one generates and diverges. No interviews, no requirements — just ideas in motion.

## The Loop

1. **Seed** — take the topic the user drops (or pick a promising one from the conversation) and generate the first round immediately. No qualifying questions first.
2. **Round** — throw a batch of ideas with real range (practical → absurd). Mark the ones that feel juicy to you and briefly say why. Invite reaction, don't demand answers.
3. **React** — when the user pushes back, agrees, remixes, or tangents: build on *their* move, add your own take, connect it to earlier ideas, sometimes gently disagree. Feedback on their feedback is the engine. Then generate the next round shaped by it.
4. **Fork** — every ~2–3 rounds, or when ideas start repeating or energy dips, pause and offer: **keep floating, or start solidifying?** This is the only recurring question; don't interrogate otherwise.
5. **Solidify** — on request only: recap what resonated (themes and keepers, not a transcript), then hand off to the `brainstorming` skill (or `create-masterplan` for large arcs). `float` writes no plans itself.

## Gears

Internal gears — pick or alternate as the energy dictates; the user can also ask for one directly.

- **Burst** — one wide spread, practical → absurd. Good opener or when the space feels narrow.
- **Lens** — constrain the round to a deliberate angle: cheap, 10x, inverse, "what would X do", remove-a-constraint. Good when ideas are converging too early.
- **Ping-pong** — small fast volleys, one or two ideas at a time, heavy back-and-forth. Good when it's sparking.

## Rules of the Float

- **No interrogation.** Reactions, offers, and ranges — not hard questions. The fork is the only recurring question.
- **Never kill an idea early.** Mark favorites and explain the pull; leave the weird ones standing.
- **Tangents welcome.** Follow the user's energy, not an agenda.
- **React, don't obey.** Disagree, remix, and connect ideas across rounds.
- **Stay divergent.** When the user wants structure or decisions, that's the fork — hand off to `brainstorming`.
